<p>Despite being mathematical systems, deep learning models are not systematically approached. Instead, they are understood in an ad-hoc manner -- every particular feature or insight is derived independently of others. Proper analysis requires various abstractions. Mathematically, we are interested in how functions feed into each other, how they are parallelized, and how functions relate to training and inference forms. Practically, we are interested in the resource costs given some mathematical goal, allowing us to find the optimal execution strategy on parallelized GPU hardware, and even to engage in systematized software-hardware co-design. Typical approaches struggle to consider these different lenses.</p>

<p>Category theory's tools for studying composition and abstraction are well-suited to the challenge of formally understanding deep learning models. It allows us to work with an abstract, mathematical model, and layer on meaning as required. This meaning could be the inference (KV-cache) form of a model, the training (backpropagated) form, or assignment to logical and physical hardware levels. Furthermore, category theory allows us to develop a rigorous diagrammatic language which reflects these abstractions. In the era of agentic mathematics, this is especially critical. Proofs are cheap, but nonetheless require an appropriate <i>language</i> in which to operate, which category theory gladly offers.</p>

<p>Our methods have been successfully applied to systematically derive hardware-aware <i>FlashAttention</i> in <a class="blue-link" href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=pF2ukh7HxA"><i>FlashAttention on a Napkin</i></a>. These methods have been applied to quickly generate a kernel for an <a class="blue-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09326">attention variant</a>. The underlying mathematics is formalized in our paper <a class="blue-link" href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=GiO8eom0jD"><i>Weaves, Wires, and Morphisms</i></a>, which is reflected in two open-source packages <a class="blue-link" href="https://github.com/mit-zardini-lab/pyncd">PyNCD</a> and <a class="blue-link" href="https://github.com/mit-zardini-lab/tsncd">TSNCD</a>.</p>
